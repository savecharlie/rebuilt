Subject: arXiv:2609.18036 — your three numbers reproduce; a question about the depth they are spent at

Dear Professor Chao and colleagues,

I read the island-arc volcanism preprint and rebuilt its numbers from the GCMT catalogue. I want to
start with what held, because it did: taking your own criterion that thrust means Mrr > 0, and the
Dahlen factor of 1000 that you quote (so |dEg| = 1000 x Es = M0/20), the 1976-2020 catalogue gives
11.8 GW of seismic power, 9.4 TW of thrust Eg release and 2.3 TW of normal-faulting Eg gain,
against your ~10 GW, ~10 TW and ~2 TW. Using your Sumatra figure of 3e21 J as the calibration
instead gives 14.3 and 3.5 TW. I could not shake any of the three.

My question is not about how large the number is. It is about the depth it is spent at.

The mechanism in section 4 assigns Morgan et al.'s 11-14 TW of descending-limb Eg dissipation to
the thrust earthquakes. In the same catalogue, weighted by seismic moment, 76.5% of the thrust
moment lies above 30 km, 95.0% above 70 km, and the moment-weighted mean centroid depth is 38.4 km.
So the proposed converter operates within roughly the top two per cent of the mantle's depth range.

On the supply side, with 3.0 km^2/yr of plate subducted, 100 km of thermal lithosphere and
drho = 50 kg/m^3, the gravitational power released by slab descent through the top z km is
drho g z Vdot: 0.33 TW by 70 km, 0.47 TW by 100 km, 3.1 TW by 660 km, and 13.5 TW for the whole
mantle. That last figure is my check that the arithmetic is right, since it reproduces the 11-14 TW
you cite - but it is a whole-mantle number. Within the depth range where 95% of the thrust moment
sits, 0.33 TW has been released.

I recognise that your dEg is not this quantity: equation (2) integrates the coseismic displacement
field over the whole Earth, and is a change in the Earth's gravitational self-energy, not a
local budget. That is exactly what I find hard, and it may be where I am wrong. The argument seems
to need dEg to be whole-Earth in magnitude and local in deposition at the arc, and I could not see
in the text what carries it from one to the other. If there is a transport mechanism I have missed,
I would genuinely like to know it.

Here is the part I think is a gift and not an objection: island-arc magma production, at
1.6-8 km^3/yr and 5.15 GJ/m^3 of sensible plus latent heat, requires 0.3-1.3 TW. The 0.33 TW
that does pass through the seismogenic depth range is already inside that band. Your mechanism
may be right at a budget thirty times smaller than the one claimed for it, and it would then need
no appeal to whole-mantle dissipation at all.

I also tried to test your statement that the release manifests in the surface heat flow of
Fig. 2(a), using the 2024 Global Heat Flow Database. I failed. Integrating the excess around
thrust seismicity gave 6.0 TW and around normal-faulting seismicity 6.2 TW - indistinguishable,
where your model predicts +10 and -2 - so the instrument was reading the continent/ocean contrast
and not a plate-boundary signal. A second attempt normalising oceanic points by seafloor age
inferred from water depth over-predicted by 56 mW/m^2 globally and failed too. I mention this only
so you know I could not make that test work in either direction.
